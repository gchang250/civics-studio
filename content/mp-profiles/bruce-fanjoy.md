Bruce Fanjoy was born in Toronto around 1964, grew up in Saint John, Fredericton and Halifax, took a commerce degree at Dalhousie and an MBA at Saint Mary's, and worked in business and marketing — including as a director of marketing at Deloitte — before stepping back for parenting and volunteer work. In April 2025 he defeated Pierre Poilievre in Carleton, a seat the Conservative leader had held since 2004, by 43,846 votes to 39,333: a 4,513-vote margin and a swing of more than twelve points. He has said he and his team had been working the ground for two years to get there.

He is the rarest kind of MP in this Parliament: a first-term backbencher whose election was itself the story. That creates a specific accountability question, which is what these entries are for. An MP who beat a party leader on the strength of a local ground campaign has more personal mandate than most rookies and, in a House where the government's margins run to single digits, more actual leverage. The record below is the beginning of an answer about what he does with it. The Voting Record section is drawn from Parliament's live vote data.
