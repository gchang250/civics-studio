Marilène Gill is an author, educator and former trade unionist who has represented Quebec's North Shore since 2015 — the riding now carries the name Côte-Nord—Kawawachikamach—Nitassinan, reflecting the Innu and Naskapi communities within it. She held it in 2025 with 43.7% of the vote. Since 2021 she has been the Bloc Québécois deputy whip and critic for Indigenous and northern affairs. She is married to fellow Bloc MP Xavier Barsalou-Duval; the two are among the small number of married couples to sit together in the Commons.

Her riding gives her file real weight: it is one of the largest and most remote in the province, with a coastal fishery, a resource economy, and several Indigenous nations whose relationship with Ottawa runs through her committee work.
