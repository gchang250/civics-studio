Jean-Yves Duclos is an economist by training and by career: a BA from the University of Alberta, graduate degrees from the London School of Economics, head of the economics department at Université Laval, and president-elect of the Canadian Economics Association before he entered politics. He won Québec Centre in 2015 by exactly 1,000 votes over the NDP incumbent — the first Liberal to take the riding since 1984 — and has turned it into an increasingly safe seat, winning 49.5% in 2025.

He held four cabinet portfolios under Justin Trudeau in a row: Families, Children and Social Development from 2015, President of the Treasury Board from 2019, Health from 2021, and Public Services and Procurement from 2023 until the change of government in 2025. He is not in Mark Carney's cabinet. He now chairs the Standing Committee on Public Safety and National Security.

An ex-minister on the backbench is one of the more revealing subjects for a voting tracker, because the whip's hold is looser and the incentives to stand out are stronger, yet the record almost always shows continued loyalty. The one place his file is genuinely contested is the Quebec jurisdiction question, where a federalist Quebec City MP has to vote against motions that most of the province's other members support.
