Jamil Jivani grew up in Brampton, worked as a dishwasher and line cook before university, took a law degree at Yale in 2013, and practised corporate law at Torys before turning to writing and broadcasting. He founded the Policing Literacy Initiative in 2013, served on the board of the Children's Aid Society of Toronto, and in December 2019 became Ontario's first Advocate for Community Opportunities and a special adviser to Premier Doug Ford, resigning in 2022 after publicly criticizing a cabinet minister. He was elected in the March 2024 Durham by-election, succeeding Erin O'Toole, and re-elected in 2025 with 49.8%.

He describes himself as a Black conservative and a proponent of national conservatism, and has built a public project — "Restore the North," launched in September 2025 — around campus outreach to young men. That is an unusual base for a Canadian MP and it makes his record interesting in two directions: whether the national-conservative framing translates into distinctive votes, and whether an MP whose main work is movement-building shows up for the ordinary business of the House. On the auto file, sitting next to Oshawa, he shows up. Elsewhere the attendance record is patchier. The Voting Record section below is drawn from Parliament's live vote data.
