On 6 December 1989 Nathalie Provost was a 23-year-old engineering student at the École Polytechnique de Montréal when Marc Lépine entered her classroom. She was shot four times, in the head and leg, and told other wounded students to play dead. She survived. Fourteen women did not.

In the decades since she became an engineer in Quebec's civil service, a mother of four, a co-founder and spokesperson for PolySeSouvient, and one of the country's most persistent voices for firearms control — including a stint as vice-chair of the Canadian Firearms Advisory Committee from 2017 to 2019, which she left because she judged the government's approach too weak. She announced her Liberal candidacy on 21 March 2025, won Châteauguay—Les Jardins-de-Napierville with 45% and a margin of more than 10,000 votes, and was appointed Secretary of State (Nature) on 13 May 2025.

That last detail is the interesting one for a voting-record tracker. She entered Parliament as the country's best-known advocate on one issue and was handed a portfolio on a different one. The entries below therefore test her on both: the conservation file she now owns, and the violence-and-hate legislation that brought her into public life.
