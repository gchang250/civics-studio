Rhéal Fortin is a lawyer who worked in a Laval factory from 1977 to 1985 before studying law at the University of Sherbrooke and building a practice in Saint-Jérôme. He was elected in Rivière-du-Nord in 2015 and, within days, was thrust into the party's top job: he served as interim leader of the Bloc Québécois from October 2015 until Martine Ouellet took over in March 2017, holding the caucus together through the low point that followed the party's near-wipeout. He held his seat in 2025 with 43.9% of the vote.

He is now the Bloc's justice critic, which makes his record unusually legible: the justice file produces recorded votes on exactly the issues he campaigns on — the Criminal Code, the treatment of Quebec's secularism law, the notwithstanding clause. Where many MPs' signature promises never reach a division, his do.
