Scott Reid is one of the longest-serving members of the House, first elected in 2000 for the Canadian Alliance and continuously since. He now represents Lanark—Frontenac in eastern Ontario, which he held in 2025 with 50.4% of the vote. An author and journalist before politics — he wrote books on Quebec partition and official bilingualism — he served as the Conservatives' democratic-institutions critic and as deputy Government House Leader under Harper.

He is unusual in the caucus for describing himself as "more libertarian than conservative," and he has the record to back the label: in 2017 he was the only Conservative MP to vote for cannabis legalization. That independent streak is worth keeping in mind, because it means his votes are not a safe bet to follow the whip on questions of individual liberty — though on the economic and procedural votes below, his libertarian instincts and the party line largely coincide. His deepest expertise is parliamentary procedure, which makes the Standing Orders fight the most on-brand test in his record. The Voting Record section is drawn from Parliament's live vote data.
