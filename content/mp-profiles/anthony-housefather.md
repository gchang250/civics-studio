Anthony Housefather was mayor of Côte Saint-Luc for a decade — first elected in 2005 with 75% of the vote — before winning Mount Royal, one of the most historically Liberal seats in the country, in 2015. He held it in 2025 with 51.1% of the vote against a strong Conservative challenge. In July 2024 the Prime Minister appointed him adviser on Canada's Jewish community and antisemitism, the role he is now most identified with.

That year also saw the most-watched moment of his career: after the Liberal caucus supported an NDP motion on Palestinian statehood, Housefather publicly weighed crossing to the Conservatives, then decided to stay, saying his "greatest value to Canadians is staying in caucus to advocate for a centrist classical Liberal Party." That episode is why his votes are worth tracking — he is a self-identified centrist who has openly contemplated leaving, which makes his continued alignment with the government a choice rather than a reflex.
