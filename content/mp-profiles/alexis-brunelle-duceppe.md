Alexis Brunelle-Duceppe has represented Lac-Saint-Jean since 2019. He is the son of former Bloc leader Gilles Duceppe, which is the first thing most coverage mentions and close to the least interesting thing about his record. He was re-elected in 2025 with 46.2% and a margin of more than 9,500 votes, and now carries two portfolios: immigration, refugees, citizenship and human rights, plus foreign affairs and international development.

The human-rights work is where he has been most independent of the party's usual preoccupations. He has sponsored motions on Tibetan self-determination and on an Iranian plot against former MP Irwin Cotler, pressed the government on China's treatment of Uyghurs, and was barred from entering both China and Russia in 2021 as a result. That record makes his file a useful test of whether a nationalist party's foreign-affairs critic votes on principle or on jurisdiction when the two point different directions — and on Bill C-3, they did.
