Michael Chong has represented his Ontario riding since 2004 and is best known for the Reform Act, a 2015 law that gave elected caucuses (not just party leaders) the formal power to review leadership and decide on admitting or expelling MPs. It's one of the only pieces of Canadian legislation aimed directly at the whip-pressure dynamic this tracker measures.

That history makes Chong's own voting record worth reading closely: a legislator who built a career arguing MPs should have more room to act independently of their leader is a natural test case for whether his own ballots ever diverge from the Conservative Party's stated position in the data below.
