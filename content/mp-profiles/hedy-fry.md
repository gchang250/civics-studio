Hedy Fry was born in San Fernando, Trinidad, trained in medicine at the Royal College of Surgeons in Dublin, and immigrated to Canada in 1970, practising at St. Paul's Hospital in Vancouver for 23 years and holding leadership posts in the medical associations. In 1993, on her first run for any office, she defeated the sitting Prime Minister, Kim Campbell, in Vancouver Centre — the fifth person in Canadian history to unseat a prime minister and the first to do it on a first attempt.

She has now won eleven consecutive elections, taking 55.2% in April 2025, and holds the record as Canada's longest-serving female MP. She was Parliamentary Secretary for National Health and Welfare from 1993, then Secretary of State for Multiculturalism and Status of Women from 1996 to 2002 under Jean Chrétien, chaired the Status of Women committee from 2009 to 2011, and has chaired the Standing Committee on Canadian Heritage since 2016.

Thirty-three years in the same seat produces a particular kind of record: heavily whipped, rarely surprising, and most informative on the two or three files where the member has genuine personal authority. For Fry those are health, hate and heritage, and the entries below are weighted accordingly. The Voting Record section is drawn from Parliament's live vote data.
