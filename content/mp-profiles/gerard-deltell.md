Gérard Deltell spent more than two decades in Quebec City journalism — TQS, TVA, Radio-Canada, CIRO-FM — before entering politics. He was elected to the National Assembly for the ADQ in 2008, led that party from 2009, and carried it into the merger that created the Coalition Avenir Québec, serving as CAQ house leader until 2014. He moved to federal politics in 2015 and has held Louis-Saint-Laurent ever since, taking 44.9% in 2025 against a seven-point decline.

He is the Conservative Party's most experienced Quebec operator and has held its heaviest jobs: Quebec lieutenant, finance critic, Treasury Board critic, and Opposition House Leader under Erin O'Toole from 2020 to 2022. That makes him the test case for whether the federal Conservative platform can be voted in Quebec without contradiction. On energy it can, expensively — he votes for pipelines in a province whose other MPs vote against them. On Quebec's cultural distinctiveness, the record below shows where the two commitments collide.
