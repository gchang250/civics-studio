Karina Gould was 28 when she unseated a Conservative incumbent in Burlington in 2015, and 29 when she became the youngest woman ever appointed to a Canadian cabinet. She held four portfolios under Justin Trudeau — Democratic Institutions, International Development, Families, Children and Social Development, and finally Government House Leader from 2023 to 2025 — and signed the early learning agreements built around $10-a-day child care. She ran for the Liberal leadership in January 2025 on a platform that included exploring a universal basic income and adjusting the carbon price, placing third behind Mark Carney and Chrystia Freeland with just over 3% of the vote. She won Burlington for a fourth time in April 2025 with over 55%.

She is now chair of the Standing Committee on Finance, which is a notable landing place for a former minister and leadership candidate: influential over the government's fiscal legislation, but on the backbench rather than in cabinet. That makes her record worth reading as a test of whether a leadership candidate's distinctive commitments survive the return to caucus discipline, or quietly fold back into the government's line.
