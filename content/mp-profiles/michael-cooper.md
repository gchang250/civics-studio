Michael Cooper is a civil litigator from St. Albert who was called to the Alberta bar in 2010 and elected to the Commons five years later. He has held the riding, now St. Albert—Sturgeon River, through four elections, and won it in 2025 with 64.0% — one of the larger margins in the country. He serves as Shadow Minister for Democratic Reform and as vice-chair of the Standing Committee on Procedure and House Affairs, which puts him at the centre of every dispute over how the House governs itself.

His career includes an episode he has never fully lived down: in 2019 he read from the Christchurch shooter's manifesto during committee testimony on online hate, and Andrew Scheer removed him from the Justice committee over it. He also attended the 2022 Freedom Convoy protest. Those facts belong in any honest account of him, and they cut against a portfolio whose currency is procedural credibility. What the record below tests is narrower and more answerable: whether an MP who argues that the government is degrading Parliament votes that way when the Standing Orders are actually on the floor.
