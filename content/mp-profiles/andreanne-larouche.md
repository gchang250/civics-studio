Andréanne Larouche has represented Shefford, in Quebec's Eastern Townships, since 2019, and is the Bloc Québécois critic for seniors, the status of women and gender equality; in the 45th Parliament she was elected vice-chair of the Standing Committee on the Status of Women. Her 2025 result is the most precarious in this group: she held the seat with 40.1% — just 571 votes ahead of the Liberal candidate's 39.3%, one of the narrowest Bloc holds in the province.

That margin is worth keeping in view when reading her record, because it means she represents a genuinely contested riding rather than a Bloc fortress, and her votes are cast with a serious Liberal challenger next door. Her portfolio — pensions, women's equality, gender-based violence — produces a clear test on Bill C-16, and a more awkward one on affordability, where the caucus's jurisdictional reflex cuts against its own cost-of-living pitch.
